Decision intelligence

The Intelligence Behind Every Security Decision.

Finding a threat is only the beginning. CyberActive AI’s decision architecture is designed to evaluate what a threat affects, how it could spread, what each response would cost, and what policy allows, before any action is taken.

Nine factors

What Every Decision Weighs.

Advanced AI can identify and analyze a threat. Deciding how to respond in a live AI environment also depends on infrastructure, consequence, and authority.

01

Threat severity

How confident and how serious the detection is.

02

Affected infrastructure

Which assets are touched or reachable.

03

Workload dependencies

Which workloads rely on those assets.

04

Potential propagation

Where the threat can move next, and how likely that is.

05

Operational consequences

What a response would interrupt or degrade.

06

Business & financial impact

Value at risk, service commitments, and recovery cost.

07

Available response options

Actions the integrated controls can actually perform.

08

Governance policies

What is pre-authorized, what needs approval, and by whom.

09

Recovery considerations

What it takes to return to a trusted state.

How a decision is formed

From Signal to Explainable Recommendation.

Each recommendation follows the same path, and each step leaves a record.

  1. 1

    Correlate

    Detections from many sources are joined into one situation using identity, asset, and timing context.

  2. 2

    Map

    The dependency model resolves what the threat can reach and through which privileges.

  3. 3

    Evaluate

    Propagation, operational disruption, and business impact are weighed for each candidate action.

  4. 4

    Check policy

    Options are filtered by governance policy and the customer’s authorization boundaries.

  5. 5

    Explain

    Each recommendation carries its evidence, rationale, expected impact, and required approvals.

Design principles

Decisions You Can Inspect.

Decision intelligence is designed to support operators, not to replace their judgment.

  • Recommendations are explainable, not opaque scores
  • Policy constraints apply before options are shown
  • Disruptive actions always require human approval
  • Business context comes from the operator
  • Every decision is recorded with its outcome
  • Outcomes inform future evaluations
